How to Track Down a Mystery Water Leak

Original ChadPDChee DIY reference · Updated September 2026

Water is annoyingly good at appearing somewhere other than where it started. Before cutting drywall, gather evidence about when the leak happens and what makes it change.

Start with timing

Does the leak happen continuously, only during rain, only when a fixture is used, only while a washing machine runs, or only after someone showers? Timing often separates supply leaks from drain leaks, fixture leaks, roofing issues, and condensation.

Check the obvious fixtures

Inspect sink traps, supply valves, toilet bases and tanks, dishwasher connections, washing-machine hoses, water heaters, refrigerator lines, and visible piping. Dry questionable joints with a towel and then watch for new moisture.

Use the water meter

If you suspect a pressurized plumbing leak, turn off fixtures and water-using appliances and note the meter or leak indicator. If the meter continues moving when no water should be in use, that can indicate an active supply-side leak. Confirm that automatic equipment such as humidifiers, ice makers, softeners, and irrigation is not operating.

Separate supply from drain

A supply leak can occur whether or not a fixture is being used because the pipe is under pressure. A drain leak often appears only while water is flowing through that drain. Running individual fixtures one at a time can help isolate the system involved.

Remember condensation

Cold-water piping, toilet tanks, HVAC components, and ducts can sweat in humid conditions. Condensation can mimic a plumbing leak, especially when moisture is seasonal.

Do not ignore hidden damage

Staining, swollen trim, soft drywall, musty odours, damaged flooring, or repeated wetness can indicate hidden moisture even if the visible drip has stopped. Long-running leaks can create structural damage and microbial growth.

If water is near electrical equipment, outlets, wiring, or a panel, avoid contact and shut down power only if it can be done safely. Water and energized equipment are not a troubleshooting combo.

Open finishes last, not first

Once timing and fixture testing have narrowed the likely path, a small targeted opening is better than exploratory demolition. Moisture meters, inspection cameras, and professional leak-detection tools can reduce unnecessary damage.
