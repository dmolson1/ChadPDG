Wall Anchors: Pick One That Matches the Wall and Load

Original ChadPDChee DIY reference · Updated September 2026

The question is not “which anchor is strongest?” It is “what wall do I have, what load am I supporting, and in what direction is that load trying to pull?”

Use structure when you can

For heavy, valuable, dynamic, or safety-critical loads, attaching to framing or another structural substrate is often preferable to relying only on drywall. Stud locations should be confirmed, and the fastener must be appropriate for the framing material and load.

Basic drywall anchors

Plastic expansion anchors can work for light loads when used in the material they are designed for. Self-drilling drywall anchors are convenient for light-to-moderate loads. Toggle-style anchors spread the load over a larger area behind the wall and can support more when correctly installed.

Load direction changes everything

A picture frame pulling mostly downward is different from a shelf bracket creating leverage away from the wall. Articulating television mounts, handrails, cabinets, and similar loads can impose substantial moment and dynamic forces that are poor candidates for casual drywall anchoring.

Masonry needs masonry fasteners

Concrete and masonry require anchors designed for that substrate, installed into a correctly sized and cleaned hole. Brick faces, mortar joints, hollow block, and solid concrete do not all behave the same way.

Ratings depend on conditions

Package ratings assume particular wall materials, thicknesses, installation quality, edge distances, and load types. Damaged drywall, oversized holes, moisture, previous repairs, and repeated movement can reduce capacity.

Know the consequence of failure

A decorative sign falling off the wall is annoying. A cabinet, grab bar, suspended object, or large television falling can cause serious injury. The higher the consequence, the more conservative the attachment method should be.

Do not drill blindly. Walls can contain electrical cables, plumbing, gas piping, and other services. Use appropriate locating methods and understand likely service routes before making holes.
